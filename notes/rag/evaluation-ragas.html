A RAG system has many dials: chunk size, overlap, embedding model, top-k, reranking, prompt wording. Change one and the answers change, but "the answers look better" is not a measurement. Evaluation turns that hunch into numbers, so you can tell whether a change actually helped and where the system is weak. Ragas is a library built for exactly this: scoring RAG pipelines with metrics that a language model computes for you.

Two failures, measured separately

A wrong answer has two possible causes, and they need different fixes. Either retrieval handed the model the wrong chunks, or retrieval was fine and the model wrote a bad answer from good text. A single accuracy score hides which one happened. Ragas splits the pipeline in two: metrics for the retrieval step and metrics for the generation step, so a bad number points at the stage that needs work instead of leaving you guessing.

Generation metrics

Two metrics judge the answer, given the retrieved context:

  • Faithfulness asks whether every claim in the answer is supported by the retrieved context. It is the direct measure of grounding: a low score means the model is inventing or drawing on outside knowledge, which is the failure RAG exists to prevent.
  • Answer relevancy asks whether the answer actually addresses the question. A response can be perfectly faithful to the context and still miss the point, rambling or answering a nearby question instead of the one asked.

Retrieval metrics

Two metrics judge what retrieval brought back, usually against a reference answer or a known ground truth:

  • Context precision asks how much of the retrieved context is actually relevant, and whether the relevant chunks rank near the top. A low score means you are padding the prompt with noise.
  • Context recall asks whether the context contains everything needed to answer. A low score means the passage with the answer never got retrieved, so no amount of prompting can save the answer.

Reading the scores together

The metrics are most useful as a set, because their pattern localizes the fault. Low context recall points at chunking or retrieval: the answer is not in what you fetched. Good recall but low precision points at top-k or reranking: the answer is there, buried in noise. Good context but low faithfulness points at the generation prompt: the model has what it needs and still strays. Low answer relevancy with everything else healthy points at the question being misread. One number tells you something is wrong; the spread tells you where.

What evaluation does not give you

These scores are computed by a language model judging another model's output, so they are estimates, not ground truth. They drift with the judge model and the prompt, and they need a set of representative questions to run against. The point is not an absolute grade. It is a stable measurement you can rerun after every change, so tuning the pipeline becomes an experiment with a result instead of a matter of taste.
