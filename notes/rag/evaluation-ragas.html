A RAG system has many dials: chunk size, overlap, embedding model, top-k, reranking, and prompt wording. Change one of them and the answers change, but "the answers look better" is not a measurement. Evaluation turns that feeling into numbers, so you see whether a change helped and where the system is weak. Ragas is a library for scoring RAG pipelines with metrics judged by a language model.

Two failures, measured separately

A wrong answer usually comes from one of two places. Retrieval gave the model the wrong chunks, or retrieval gave the model useful chunks and the model still wrote a bad answer. These failures need different fixes. A single accuracy score hides which one happened.

Ragas separates the pipeline into retrieval and generation. Retrieval metrics judge what the system fetched. Generation metrics judge the answer written from that context. This way, a bad score points to the part of the pipeline that needs work.

Generation metrics

Two metrics judge the answer against the retrieved context:

  • Faithfulness asks whether the claims in the answer are supported by the retrieved context. It measures grounding directly. A low score means the model is inventing, guessing, or using knowledge outside the supplied text.
  • Answer relevancy asks whether the answer addresses the question. An answer might be fully supported by the context and still miss the point by answering a nearby question or adding unrelated detail.

Retrieval metrics

Two metrics judge the context that retrieval brought back:

  • Context precision asks how much of the retrieved context is relevant, and whether the relevant chunks appear near the top. A low score means the prompt is being filled with noise.
  • Context recall asks whether the retrieved context contains the information needed to answer. A low score means the answer was missing from the fetched chunks, so better prompting will not fix the result.

Reading the scores together

The metrics are most useful together because their pattern points to the failure. Low context recall points at chunking or retrieval: the answer is missing from what the system fetched. Good recall but low precision points at top-k or reranking: the answer is present, but buried in noise. Good context but low faithfulness points at generation: the model had the needed text and still strayed from it. Low answer relevancy with healthy retrieval and faithfulness points at the model misreading the question.

One number tells you something is wrong. The spread of scores tells you where to look.

What evaluation does not give you

Ragas scores are estimates. A language model is judging another model's output, so the scores depend on the judge model, the judging prompt, and the questions you test with. They should not be treated as perfect truth or as an absolute grade.

Their value is repeatability. With a representative test set, you rerun the same evaluation after each change and compare the results. That turns RAG tuning from a matter of taste into an experiment with a visible outcome.
