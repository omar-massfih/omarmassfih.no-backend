How do you get a piece of information to every machine in a large, shifting group, when there is no central coordinator and machines come and go? The obvious idea, a single source telling everyone directly, has real problems: that source becomes a bottleneck and a single point of failure, and it has to keep track of who everyone is. There is a lovelier approach, borrowed from how rumors spread among people.

Everybody tells a few

In this approach, each machine that learns something new tells just a few other machines, picked at random. Those machines each tell a few more, who tell a few more, and so on. Nobody tells everybody; everybody tells a few. It looks almost lazy, yet it works extraordinarily well.

Because every newly-informed machine spreads the news further, the number that know grows explosively, doubling and doubling. So the information reaches the entire group remarkably fast, even a very large group, through many small, overlapping conversations rather than one big broadcast.

Why this is a good design

There is no coordinator to overload or to fail, because every machine does the same small job. That makes it naturally robust: if some machines are down or some messages are lost, the information still finds its way around, because there are many overlapping paths and it keeps being re-shared by whoever has it. And no machine needs to know the whole group; each only needs a handful of others to gossip with. Simplicity and resilience come together.

The trade-off

It is not instant or perfectly orderly. Information ripples outward over several rounds instead of arriving everywhere at once, so for a brief window some machines know and others do not. You get agreement eventually, not immediately. For spreading things like which machines are alive, a configuration change, or group membership across a big, changing set of machines, that short delay is usually a fine price to pay for robustness and having no one in charge.

The point

To spread information across many machines without a central coordinator, let each one tell a few random others, exactly like a rumor. It reaches the whole group fast, keeps working when parts fail or messages drop, and needs no machine to be in charge or to know everyone. The cost is only that agreement arrives in a quick ripple rather than all at once.
