Suppose you want to limit how fast something can happen, requests to a service, messages sent, actions taken, to keep a system from being overwhelmed. The crude way is to allow exactly one every fixed interval, no exceptions. That is often too rigid, because real usage does not arrive in a perfectly even drip. It comes in bursts, and a short burst followed by quiet is usually harmless; what you actually want to stop is a sustained flood.

A bucket of tokens

There is an elegant mechanism that captures exactly this distinction: the token bucket. Picture a bucket that holds tokens. Every action must take a token to proceed; no token, no action, so it waits or is turned away. The bucket refills at a steady rate, a token every so often, up to some maximum it can hold.

That is the whole mechanism. Its behavior, though, is exactly what you want.

Bursts allowed, floods capped

Watch what it permits. If actions have been quiet for a while, the bucket fills up. Then a sudden burst can grab many tokens at once and all proceed immediately, so short bursts are welcome, up to the size of the bucket. Real, well-behaved traffic that arrives in clumps sails through.

But you cannot keep it up. Once the bucket drains, you are limited to the steady refill rate, because that is the only speed at which new tokens appear. So over any long stretch, the average rate cannot exceed the refill rate, no matter how you time things. Bursts in the short term, bounded in the long term.

Two knobs

The beauty is that you get two independent knobs. The refill rate sets the sustainable long-run average, how fast the thing is allowed to go over time. The bucket size sets how big a burst you will tolerate, how much slack can build up during quiet periods to be spent all at once. Tune them separately to match what you actually want to allow.

This fits how real traffic looks, bursty in the short term and bounded in the long term, far better than forcing everything into a rigid, even trickle.

Worth remembering

To throttle something while still allowing natural bursts, use a bucket of tokens that refills at a steady rate: each action spends a token, and empty means wait. The refill rate caps the long-run average, and the bucket's size sets how large a burst you permit. Two simple knobs give you a limit that bends for real, clumpy traffic instead of fighting it.
