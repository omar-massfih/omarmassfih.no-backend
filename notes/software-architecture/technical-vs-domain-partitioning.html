When you break a system into parts, there are two fundamentally different ways to draw the lines, and they lead to very different systems. The choice looks like an organizing detail. It is actually one of the most consequential decisions you make, because it decides what a typical change touches.

Two ways to slice

You can slice by technical role. All the screen and presentation code goes in one part, all the business rules in another, all the database code in a third. Each part is defined by the kind of work it does, stacked in horizontal layers.

Or you can slice by business area. Everything about orders goes in one part, everything about billing in another, everything about shipping in a third. Each part owns one slice of the business from top to bottom, screen to data.

What a change touches

Here is the consequence that matters. Real changes almost always arrive shaped like the business: "change how orders work." Now ask where that change lands under each slicing.

If you sliced by technical role, that one business change is smeared across every layer. You touch the order screen in the presentation part, the order rules in the business part, and the order data in the database part, three separate places, perhaps owned by three separate teams who now must coordinate. If you sliced by business area, the same change stays inside the one "orders" part. One team, one place. The way the system is cut decides whether a normal change is contained or scattered.

The flip side

Technical slicing is not without merit. It groups similar code together, so all the database code lives in one tidy place, which is easy to find and easy to police with tools that check the layers are respected. Its weakness is exactly the scattering above.

Domain slicing keeps each business change in one place, but it demands discipline: you have to keep the business areas from bleeding into one another, or the clean boundaries dissolve and you are back to a tangle. Neither slicing is free; each asks something different of you.

The point

A system can be divided by technical role or by business area, and the choice quietly determines what a typical change touches. Slice by technical role and a single business change spreads across every layer; slice by business area and it stays put, at the cost of guarding the boundaries. Since change almost always arrives shaped like the business, domain slicing tends to age better as a system grows, which is worth knowing before you draw the first line.
